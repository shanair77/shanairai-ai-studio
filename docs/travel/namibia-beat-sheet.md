**Shot types:** **AI-K** Kling 4.0 hero clip · **AI-M** MiniMax atmosphere clip · **LAYER** painted plate animated in code · **CODE** drawn in Remotion

**The idea of the episode:** life in a land with almost no rain, straight after the Okavango's water. It runs from **the desert** (red dunes, dead trees that never rot, life that drinks fog, a plant with two leaves that lives for centuries) to **the coast** (shipwrecks and seals) to **the north** (Etosha's waterholes), its **history**, and the darkest skies in Africa.

As with the last two episodes, **every fact below was checked before writing** (sources at the end).

## Beats

### 0 · Hook — CODE (Deadvlei)
- **Picture:** A pen draws a white clay pan under a red dune and blue sky, then three black, bare trees; a "~900 yrs" tag on a string swings onto the biggest tree and settles.
- **Narration:** "These trees died hundreds of years ago, and they're still standing."
- **On screen:** "Deadvlei · Namibia" written in as the tag settles.

### 1 · Title — CODE
- **Picture:** The Africa map draws in; the season route runs Morocco → … → Okavango → Namibia; the stamp lands.
- **Narration:** "This is Postcards from Africa. Stop six: Namibia."
- **On screen:** Location card with **Namibia's flag** (blue, red and green with the golden sun); stamp "STOP 06 · NAMIBIA".

### 2 · The red dunes — AI-K (hero)
- **Picture:** Sunrise at Sossusvlei. The traveler, seen from behind, walks along a knife-edge dune crest; one side glows orange, the other lies in deep shadow; footprints trail behind her.
- **Narration:** "The Namib is one of the oldest deserts on Earth, and around Sossusvlei its red dunes rise more than three hundred metres high."
- **Fact card:** "Big Daddy, one of the tallest dunes here, is about 325 metres tall."

### 3 · Deadvlei — LAYER (push)
- **Picture:** The white clay pan of Deadvlei, black dead camel-thorn trees, a towering orange dune behind under a deep blue sky.
- **Narration:** "When the dunes cut off the river, the trees here died. But the air is so dry that, centuries later, they still haven't rotted away."
- **Fact card:** "The trees are thought to be around 600 to 900 years old."

### 4 · Drinking the fog — LAYER
- **Picture:** Cool morning fog rolling in from the Atlantic over the dunes; a beetle cut-out on the dune crest, head down, with droplets on its back.
- **Narration:** "Fog from the cold Atlantic drifts over the dunes, and some beetles tilt their backs into it to catch drops of water to drink."
- **Fact card:** "The Namib Sand Sea became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2013."

### 5 · Welwitschia — LAYER
- **Picture:** A welwitschia sprawled on gravel plains: two long, split, ribbon-like leaves curling across the ground; distant mountains.
- **Narration:** "The welwitschia plant grows just two leaves in its whole life, and the biggest are thought to be well over a thousand years old."

### 6 · The Skeleton Coast — AI-M (atmosphere)
- **Picture:** A rusted shipwreck on a foggy beach, waves breaking around it, a few seals on the sand. No people.
- **Narration:** "On the Skeleton Coast, thick fog and rough seas have wrecked ships for centuries."
- **Fact card:** "The name comes partly from the whale and seal bones that once covered its beaches."

### 7 · Cape Cross — LAYER
- **Picture:** A rocky shore packed with Cape fur seals, pups and adults, surf spraying.
- **Narration:** "Further up the coast at Cape Cross, up to two hundred thousand fur seals gather to breed."

### 8 · Etosha — AI-K (hero)
- **Picture:** The traveler, seen from behind in an open safari vehicle, watches elephants, zebras and springbok drinking at a waterhole; beyond, the vast white salt pan shimmers to the horizon.
- **Narration:** "In Etosha National Park, animals gather at waterholes along the edge of a salt pan almost five thousand square kilometres wide."
- **Fact card:** "Floodlit waterholes at the rest camps let you watch animals after dark, sometimes even rare black rhino."

### 9 · Swakopmund — LAYER (history card)
- **Picture:** A quiet street of pastel German colonial-era buildings by the sea. No people.
- **Narration:** "In Swakopmund, German-era buildings recall Namibia's colonial past."
- **History card:** "Between 1904 and 1908, German colonial forces killed tens of thousands of Herero and Nama people. In 2021, Germany recognised it as genocide." The beat holds for 9.5 s so the card can be read.

### 10 · The darkest skies — AI-K (hero)
- **Picture:** Night in the NamibRand reserve: the traveler, seen from behind, sits on a dune crest under a blazing Milky Way.
- **Narration:** "And at night, Namibia has some of the darkest, starriest skies on Earth."
- **Fact card:** "In 2012, the NamibRand Nature Reserve became Africa's first International Dark Sky Reserve."

### 11 · Traveler's notes — CODE
- **Notes:**
  - **Best time:** May–October (dry season), when Etosha's animals crowd the waterholes.
  - **Getting around:** long, empty roads; most travellers self-drive or join a small tour.
  - **Money:** Namibian dollar, equal to the South African rand, which is also accepted.
  - **Language:** English (official), plus many local languages.
  - **Respect:** stay on marked tracks, never touch the trees of Deadvlei, ask before photographing people.
- **Narration:** "Come between May and October, carry water on every drive, and leave nothing behind but footprints."

### 12 · Next stop — CODE
- **Picture:** The route line runs on from Namibia to the next stop, with a doodle.
- **Narration:** "Next stop: Cape Town."
- **On screen:** "STOP 07 → CAPE TOWN" with a Table Mountain doodle; stamp "VISITED · NAMIBIA".

**Narration:** about 250 words across 13 beats. **Estimated running time:** ~105–115 s.
