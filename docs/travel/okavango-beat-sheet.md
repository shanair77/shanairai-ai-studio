**The idea of the episode:** water where you don't expect it. A river runs into the Kalahari Desert and spreads into a green maze instead of reaching the sea. Its flood arrives in the dry season, and rain is so precious that the country's money is named after it. The episode moves from **the water** (the delta, the flood, the mokoro) to **the wildlife** (elephants, Moremi) to **the land and its people** (Tsodilo's rock art, the pula, a campfire).

As with Victoria Falls, **every fact below was checked before writing** (sources at the end).

## Beats

### 0 · Hook — CODE (the river that never reaches the sea)
- **Picture:** Rain falls on the Angolan highlands; a pen draws the river down the page; it fans out like an open hand across the Kalahari's sand and fades away, with islands appearing between the channels.
- **Narration:** "This river flows into a desert, and never reaches the sea."
- **On screen:** "Okavango Delta · Botswana" written in as the river fans out.

### 1 · Title — CODE
- **Picture:** The Africa map draws in; the season route runs Morocco → Egypt → Tanzania → Victoria Falls → Okavango; the stamp lands.
- **Narration:** "This is Postcards from Africa. Stop five: the Okavango Delta."
- **On screen:** Location card with **Botswana's flag** (light blue with a black band edged in white); stamp "STOP 05 · OKAVANGO".

### 2 · From the air — AI-K (hero)
- **Picture:** The traveler, seen from behind, looks out of a small plane's window at the delta below: winding blue channels, round green islands, palm trees, a herd crossing the water.
- **Narration:** "From above, the Okavango is a maze of channels, lagoons and islands, spread out across the Kalahari."
- **Fact card:** "In 2014, it became UNESCO's 1,000th World Heritage Site."

### 3 · The upside-down flood — LAYER (push)
- **Picture:** Clear floodwater spreading across dry golden grassland under a cloudless sky, filling hollows and turning them into pools; antelope wading at the edge.
- **Narration:** "Its water falls as rain in the highlands of Angola, and takes months to get here. So the flood peaks in the middle of the dry season."
- **Fact card:** "The rains fall around January; the flood reaches its height in Botswana around June to August."

### 4 · By mokoro — AI-K (hero)
- **Picture:** The traveler sits low in a narrow canoe gliding through a channel of papyrus and water lilies; a local poler stands at the back with a long pole, painted as a loose figure with no facial detail.
- **Narration:** "The best way to explore is by mokoro: a narrow canoe, pushed through the reeds by a local poler."
- **Say it:** "Dumela — hello"

### 5 · Elephant country — AI-M (atmosphere)
- **Picture:** An elephant family walks along a palm-dotted island at dawn, egrets lifting off the water around them. No people. (A different picture from the Chobe river crossing in episode 04.)
- **Narration:** "Botswana is home to about a third of all Africa's elephants: around a hundred and thirty thousand."

### 6 · Moremi — LAYER
- **Picture:** Shallow floodplain at golden hour; a red lechwe antelope bounds through the water as a foreground cut-out, spray flying, while a pack of wild dogs watches from the far bank.
- **Narration:** "In Moremi Game Reserve, red lechwe bound through the shallows, and rare African wild dogs hunt in packs."
- **Fact card:** "Moremi is one of the best places in Africa to see wild dogs."

### 7 · Tsodilo Hills — LAYER
- **Picture:** A rock face in warm light covered in red-ochre paintings of eland, giraffe and handprints; the hills rise behind; the traveler small at a respectful distance with a local guide.
- **Narration:** "In the northwest, the Tsodilo Hills hold more than four thousand five hundred rock paintings, some made by ancestors of the San people."
- **Fact card:** "Local communities still treat the hills as a sacred place. Visit with a guide and never touch the paintings."
- **Care notes:** the San are presented through their ancestors' art and living respect for the site, with no "primitive" framing and no costumed figures.

### 8 · Pula — LAYER
- **Picture:** A dark rain cloud rolling over the dry Kalahari, the first drops darkening the sand; a few Botswana-style coins in the foreground catch the light.
- **Narration:** "Rain is so precious here that Botswana's money is called pula, the Setswana word for rain."
- **Fact card:** "'Pula!' is also the national motto, a wish for blessing and good fortune."

### 9 · Campfire on an island — AI-K (hero)
- **Picture:** Dusk on a delta island: the traveler sits by a small campfire, seen from behind, looking across a lagoon glowing orange; hippos surface; the first stars come out.
- **Narration:** "And when the sun goes down, the delta comes alive with frogs, hippos and the call of the African fish eagle."

### 10 · Traveler's notes — CODE
- **Notes:**
  - **Best time:** June–October, when the flood is high and the dry season brings animals to the water.
  - **Getting there:** fly into Maun, the gateway town; most camps are reached by small plane.
  - **Money:** Botswana pula.
  - **Language:** English and Setswana.
  - **Respect:** give wildlife space, never feed animals, never touch rock paintings, follow your guide.
- **Narration:** "Come between June and October, fly in through Maun, and always listen to your guide."

### 11 · Next stop — CODE
- **Picture:** The route line runs on from the delta to the next stop, with a doodle.
- **Narration:** "Next stop: Namibia."
- **On screen:** "STOP 06 → NAMIBIA" with a dunes doodle; stamp "VISITED · OKAVANGO DELTA".

**Narration:** about 220 words across 12 beats. **Estimated running time:** ~100–110 s.

**Left out on purpose:** Botswana's visitor levy. The only sources are from 2017–18 and disagree about whether it was introduced, withdrawn or reintroduced, so the notes say nothing about fees.
